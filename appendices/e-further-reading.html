Appendix EFurther Reading

Start here

Palermo’s ECEN 720 course [4] is the backbone of this tutorial; its lectures on channels and link analysis [13], equalization [22], circuits [24] and clocking [34] go deeper on every chapter. The forthcoming book by Shekhar, Palermo and Hanumolu [65] covers the same ground in textbook form.

Signal integrity and systems

Dally and Poulton [14] is the classic on signaling, noise budgets and timing in digital systems. Hall and Heck [16] treat transmission lines, losses, S-parameters and channel modeling in the detail you need to replace REF with a real board. Stojanović et al. [23] show a complete adaptive PAM2/4 transceiver and why the equalization partition matters. The IEEE 802.3 backplane clauses [7] define the channels, masks and test methods of real 2.5 and 5 Gb/s Ethernet links.

Circuits

Razavi’s analog text [66] is the reference for differential pairs, noise and mismatch. His optical-communications book [40] covers CML, limiting amplifiers, CDRs and multiplexers at the transistor level, much as Open-SERDES builds them.

Clocking

Razavi’s PLL book [36] takes the charge-pump PLL from the loop equations to the VCO and divider circuits. Gardner [37] is the classic on loop dynamics and the source of most PLL design formulas. The team’s 2.4 GHz LC-VCO PLL paper [9] is the starting point for the Open-SERDES PLL.

Open-source design

The IHP SG13G2 open PDK [1], the IIC-OSIC-TOOLS image [2] and the JKU chip template [5] are everything you need to tape out; read the template tutorial before your first make all. The Purdue OpenSerDes paper [11] is the closest open-source prior art, an all-digital 2 Gb/s link in SKY130. For modeling, serdespy [3] powers the labs of Chapter 16, and PyBERT [44] is the natural next step, with a GUI and IBIS-AMI support.
